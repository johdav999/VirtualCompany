# Bare-Bones Accounting Application

## Purpose

This document describes how to turn the existing Virtual Company finance capability into a small but real accounting application for micro-companies that do not already use accounting software. The target is not a Fortnox clone and not a full ERP. It is the smallest production-grade accounting core that can maintain trustworthy books for one legal entity per Virtual Company tenant, while leaving payroll, inventory, statutory filing, advanced consolidation, and specialist accounting to later phases or external services.

The application should reuse the finance, ledger, invoice, bill, payment, reconciliation, reporting, approval, audit, and integration foundations already in the repository. It should not create a second finance subsystem and must not require Fortnox or another accounting provider to operate.

The accounting kernel must be country-agnostic. It owns universal bookkeeping concepts such as accounts, periods, balanced journals, source evidence, reconciliation, statements, corrections, and audit. Country-specific charts, taxes, invoice requirements, statutory reports, retention rules, and exports are supplied through versioned accounting-policy packs. No country, currency, account numbering scheme, or tax vocabulary may be hard-coded into the kernel.

A qualified accountant or other competent local specialist must validate a policy pack before it is represented as compliant for a country. The generic accounting application can provide bookkeeping without such a pack, but it must clearly state that country-specific tax and statutory compliance has not been configured.

## Outcome

A company should be able to:

1. Configure its fiscal year, base currency, chart of accounts, tax settings, and posting series.
2. Register customer invoices and supplier bills with source documents.
3. Post balanced, traceable journal entries for those documents.
4. Import or enter bank transactions and reconcile them against payments.
5. Enter controlled manual journals and correct errors without rewriting posted history.
6. Review the general ledger, account balances, trial balance, profit and loss statement, balance sheet, receivables, payables, and configured tax summary.
7. Close and lock an accounting period after all blocking issues are resolved.
8. Export the books and their evidence for an accountant or an external accounting provider.

## Bare-Bones Scope

### Included in the first usable release

- One legal entity per company tenant.
- One base currency per legal entity; foreign-currency source amounts may be retained, but all ledger lines are posted in base currency.
- Accrual double-entry accounting.
- Chart-of-accounts setup from a versioned template.
- Fiscal years and monthly accounting periods.
- Sequential voucher numbers within a voucher series and fiscal year.
- Draft, validate, post, reverse, and view journal entries.
- Customer invoice, supplier bill, credit note, and payment posting.
- Bank transaction import/manual entry and reconciliation.
- Configurable tax codes and a reviewable tax summary.
- Trial balance, general ledger, profit and loss statement, and balance sheet.
- Period-close checks, closing, and locking.
- Tenant isolation, role-based authorization, approvals, source-document links, business audit, and operator-visible failures.
- Country-neutral exports and optional provider adapters through the existing integration boundary.

### Deliberately excluded

- Payroll and employer reporting.
- Inventory valuation and cost accounting.
- Fixed-asset depreciation automation beyond existing simple asset support.
- Purchase ordering, order fulfilment, and full sales-order management.
- Group consolidation and intercompany elimination.
- Multi-book, multi-GAAP, or multi-entity accounting.
- Automated statutory tax filing or payment to an authority.
- Open-ended accounting rules generated by AI.
- A generic workflow engine or separate accounting microservice.

These exclusions keep the first release small without weakening the ledger. Features can be added later because they post through the same journal-entry boundary.

## Country-Agnostic Design

Country agnosticism does not mean ignoring local rules. It means isolating them from the accounting kernel so a company can select the rules that apply without changing core code.

### Accounting kernel

The kernel owns only universal concepts and invariants:

- Companies, accounts, account classes, fiscal periods, voucher series, and journals.
- Fixed-precision debit and credit amounts.
- Balanced-entry validation.
- Draft, approval, posting, reversal, and close transitions.
- Invoices, bills, payments, bank transactions, allocations, and reconciliation.
- General ledger, trial balance, profit and loss statement, and balance sheet.
- Source-document traceability, tenant isolation, authorization, audit, idempotency, and concurrency.
- Configurable tax calculation and reporting interfaces, but no built-in assumption about VAT, GST, sales tax, or account numbers.

### Accounting-policy packs

A versioned policy pack supplies local or organizational defaults such as:

- Country/region and supported legal-entity types.
- Local terminology and translations.
- One or more chart-of-accounts templates.
- Tax types, rates, effective dates, recoverability rules, tax accounts, and report mappings.
- Required invoice fields and invoice/credit-note numbering rules.
- Fiscal-year and period defaults.
- Currency, precision, rounding, and display defaults.
- Statement layouts and statutory report categories.
- Retention, lock, reopening, and evidence requirements.
- Supported bank-import and accountant-export formats.
- Validation rules and plain-English remediation messages.

Packs should be declarative wherever practical. A provider-specific exporter or unusually complex local calculation may use an adapter behind an Application contract, but it may not weaken core balance, immutability, tenant, authorization, approval, or audit invariants.

Each company selects a pack and version during setup. The effective pack version and calculated tax facts are retained with postings so installing a later pack cannot reinterpret historical books. Pack upgrades require validation, an effective date, an impact preview, and an audit record.

The system must not silently substitute a different country pack. If a required local capability is absent, the application should continue to provide the country-neutral ledger where safe while clearly marking country-specific reports or actions as unavailable.

## Architecture Decision: Native Finance Capability, Not a Provider

### Decision

The bare-bones accounting application is a first-class, integrated part of the existing Finance capability. It is not registered as another `IFinanceIntegrationProvider` alongside Fortnox.

The internal SQL Server ledger is the accounting system of record for the target micro-company. External accounting applications, banks, accountant exports, and tax tools connect through adapters around that native capability.

```text
Finance UI / Laura / workflows
              |
              v
Finance application commands and policies
              |
              v
Native accounting kernel
(accounts, journals, periods, taxes, reporting)
              |
              v
Internal SQL Server ledger — system of record
              |
       durable outbox/events
              |
              v
Optional external adapters
(Fortnox, banks, accountant exports, tax tools)
```

This is a ports-and-adapters design inside the existing modular monolith. Finance owns the business capability; integrations remain replaceable edge adapters.

### Why the internal application is not a finance provider

The existing `IFinanceIntegrationProvider` contract is designed for remote applications. It groups OAuth, connection lifecycle, synchronization, provider mapping, and external write commands. These are correct concerns for Fortnox but incorrect semantics for a local accounting engine.

| Native accounting application | External finance provider |
| --- | --- |
| Always available for an authorized tenant | Requires a configured connection |
| Owns authoritative accounting state | Owns remote provider state |
| Uses local SQL Server transactions | Uses eventually consistent network calls |
| Enforces accounting invariants directly | Maps internal models to provider contracts |
| Has no OAuth or synchronization lifecycle | Requires authentication, sync, retries, and reconciliation |
| Commits or rolls back atomically | Can produce ambiguous external outcomes |

Treating the native engine as a provider would require fake OAuth, connection, sync, mapper, and remote-write implementations. It would also force provider-shaped failure and eventual-consistency behavior onto accounting operations that should be atomic inside the local database.

The provider abstraction must therefore remain an integration boundary. It must not become the abstraction for the accounting domain itself.

### Capability ownership

Accounting work remains within the current project boundaries:

- `VirtualCompany.Domain`: accounts, account semantics, fiscal periods, voucher series, journal entries and lines, tax facts, reversals, and deterministic accounting invariants.
- `VirtualCompany.Application/Finance`: accounting commands, queries, policies, result contracts, policy-pack contracts, and capability interfaces.
- `VirtualCompany.Persistence`: accounting entity configurations and shared SQL Server persistence.
- `VirtualCompany.Persistence.Migrations`: accounting migrations and the EF Core model snapshot.
- `VirtualCompany.Infrastructure.Finance`: posting, tax calculation, reconciliation, period close, reporting, policy-pack loading, background work, and external Finance adapters.
- `VirtualCompany.Api`: authorization, company context, transport mapping, and safe problem responses.
- `VirtualCompany.Web`: accounting pages, presentation models, and typed Finance API clients.

Do not create a separate accounting infrastructure project, accounting microservice, parallel database context, or second Finance domain. Extracting a dedicated Finance-owned project is only justified later if the existing Finance assembly becomes too large and the dependency boundary is mechanically enforceable; it is not required to establish the accounting architecture.

### Native accounting application contracts

Define accounting capability contracts independently from integration-provider contracts. The exact decomposition should follow implemented use cases, but the application boundary should conceptually include:

```text
IAccountingPostingService
IAccountingJournalReadService
IAccountingPeriodService
IAccountingReportingService
IAccountingPolicyPackResolver
IAccountingExportService
```

These are not generic provider interfaces. They express native accounting operations and are implemented by Finance services over the internal ledger.

Country-policy packs implement a separate configuration/policy contract, conceptually:

```csharp
public interface IAccountingPolicyPack
{
    string PackKey { get; }
    string Version { get; }

    ChartOfAccountsTemplate ChartOfAccounts { get; }
    IReadOnlyCollection<TaxRule> TaxRules { get; }
    InvoicePolicy InvoicePolicy { get; }
    ReportingPolicy ReportingPolicy { get; }
}
```

This interface is illustrative rather than a required final shape. The production contract should use the repository's typed Application-contract conventions and should separate large concerns when doing so improves ownership and versioning.

An accounting-policy pack configures the native kernel. It does not own a connection, store the company's books, perform synchronization, or become an `IFinanceIntegrationProvider`.

### Central posting boundary

Every source of accounting activity converges on one governed posting boundary:

```text
Customer invoice ─┐
Supplier bill ─────┤
Bank payment ──────┤
Manual journal ────┼──> Accounting posting service ──> Internal ledger
Credit note ───────┤
Adjustment ────────┘
```

The posting boundary owns:

- Company and resource ownership validation.
- Open-period and reporting-lock validation.
- Account, currency, tax, and dimension validation.
- Whole-entry debit/credit balance validation.
- Source-version and idempotency validation.
- Approval and posting-actor validation.
- Concurrency-safe voucher-number allocation.
- Atomic entry, line, source-link, approval-evidence, and audit persistence.
- Stable result and failure codes.

Source-specific services and versioned posting templates build proposed entries. They do not write ledger entries directly. Existing bank reconciliation and cash-settlement posting should converge on the same boundary so the repository has one authoritative implementation of posting invariants.

### Distinct extension concepts

Keep these three concepts separate:

1. **Finance capability:** owns invoices, bills, payments, accounting, reconciliation, and financial reporting.
2. **Accounting-policy pack:** supplies versioned country or organizational charts, taxes, invoice rules, terminology, reports, and exports.
3. **Finance integration provider:** connects Finance to an external application using authentication, mapping, synchronization, durable writes, retries, and reconciliation.

Do not use the term `provider` for the native accounting engine or for a country-policy pack. Clear naming prevents local accounting operations from accidentally inheriting external-integration semantics.

### Accounting authority

For a micro-company without existing accounting software, the default and normal mode is:

```text
Accounting authority = Virtual Company internal ledger
```

If compatibility with an existing or later-adopted accounting provider is required, persist an explicit accounting-authority decision per company and accounting period. The supported states may be represented conceptually as:

- `InternalLedger`: Virtual Company owns posting and financial truth.
- `ExternalProvider`: the provider owns the authoritative books; Virtual Company holds synchronized projections and workflow state.
- `Migration`: a bounded transition in which posting is restricted while balances and sources are reconciled.

The state names are illustrative; production code should use plain user-facing language and stable typed storage values consistent with repository conventions.

One company and accounting period must never have two independent accounting authorities. Virtual Company and Fortnox must not both create authoritative postings for the same business event. Authority changes require a validated cutover, opening/trial-balance reconciliation, effective period boundary, audit record, and operator-visible result.

For the initial bare-bones release, implement `InternalLedger` as the normal production path. Do not delay the native application merely to generalize every possible external-authority workflow.

### External adapter behavior

When the internal ledger is authoritative, external adapters consume approved internal results through durable integration work:

```text
Internal ledger
      |
      +--> Fortnox export or synchronization
      +--> Bank integration
      +--> Accountant export
      +--> Country-pack authority/tax export
```

Provider writes remain outside the local accounting transaction and use the existing approval, outbox/background execution, idempotency, retry classification, safe failure, and reconciliation rules. A provider failure must not roll back or corrupt an already committed internal journal. An ambiguous provider outcome becomes reconciliation work and must not cause the internal accounting action to be posted again.

### Consequences

This decision provides:

- A trustworthy local system of record for companies without accounting software.
- Atomic accounting operations without artificial network semantics.
- One authoritative implementation of ledger invariants.
- Country agnosticism through policy packs rather than conditional logic spread through Finance.
- Optional Fortnox and future integrations without making the core dependent on them.
- Clear migration and authority rules for companies that adopt an external system later.

The main cost is that the native accounting kernel must be engineered and validated as a real financial system rather than delegating correctness to Fortnox. That cost is intentional: providing accounting to companies without another accounting application is the purpose of this capability.

## Current Repository Foundation

Virtual Company already contains much of the substrate required for this application:

- `FinanceAccount` provides tenant-owned accounts.
- `FiscalPeriod`, `LedgerEntry`, `LedgerEntryLine`, and `LedgerEntrySourceMapping` provide periods, journal headers, debit/credit lines, and source traceability.
- Ledger lines already reject negative values, zero-value lines, and a line containing both a debit and a credit.
- Ledger entries have draft/posted states, tenant-scoped unique entry numbers, and source-based idempotency indexes.
- Invoices, bills, counterparties, payments, allocations, bank accounts, bank transactions, and reconciliation records already exist.
- Cash settlement and bank reconciliation already create ledger entries with duplicate-posting protection.
- Trial-balance and financial-statement snapshot entities, statement mappings, reporting-period close logic, and drill-down reporting already exist.
- Supplier-bill ingestion, document extraction, review, approval, and optional Fortnox integration are substantial existing capabilities.
- The platform already has tenant authorization, approvals, tasks/workflows, durable background execution, audit events, and finance UI surfaces.

The implementation is therefore a hardening and completion project, not a greenfield accounting system.

The most important gaps to close are:

- One authoritative posting service and policy for every journal source.
- Proper voucher series and concurrency-safe sequential numbering instead of source-derived display identifiers.
- Immutable posted entries with reversal/correction workflows.
- Explicit account and tax-code semantics instead of stringly typed account categories.
- Posting templates for invoices, bills, payments, credits, fees, and configurable taxes.
- A safe manual-journal workbench.
- Clear separation between operational finance records, provider-synchronized projections, and the authoritative internal ledger.
- A country-agnostic accounting-policy pack contract plus configurable tax reporting.
- Accountant-oriented general-ledger, trial-balance, close, and export surfaces.
- Migration and reconciliation of historical local or provider-derived finance data when a company has any.

## Accounting Invariants

These rules are mandatory backend invariants, not UI validation or AI instructions.

### Balanced entries

For every journal entry:

```text
sum(debit in base currency) = sum(credit in base currency)
```

Amounts use fixed-precision decimal values and a single documented rounding policy. A posted entry must contain at least two lines and must not contain zero-only lines.

### Tenant ownership

The period, entry, accounts, tax codes, dimensions, source record, document, approval, and actor must belong to the same company. A route or request `companyId` is context, not proof of access.

### Open-period posting

An entry can only be posted into an existing open accounting period. Closed or reporting-locked periods reject new postings and modifications. Reopening requires a separately authorized and audited operation.

### Immutable posted history

Draft entries may be edited or discarded. Posted entries may not be edited or deleted. A correction creates a new reversing or adjusting entry linked to the original. The original remains visible.

### Sequential identity

The voucher number is allocated atomically at posting time from a company, fiscal-year, and series-specific sequence. Numbers are never reused. A failed draft does not consume a number; a committed posting does.

### Idempotent source posting

The same source action and source version must not create two postings. A stable key should include:

```text
company + posting action + source type + source id + source version
```

Replaying the same command returns the original result. A materially changed source requires a new version and normally a reversal plus replacement, not mutation of the earlier entry.

### Source evidence

Every non-manual posting links to its business source and, where applicable, its source document. Every manual entry includes an explanation and evidence or an explicitly recorded reason why no document applies.

### Deterministic authority

Balances, tax amounts, posting eligibility, account validity, period validity, approval requirements, and posting success are determined by backend accounting policies. AI may extract fields or recommend accounts, tax codes, and explanations, but it must not bypass deterministic validation or post without the configured approval.

## Target Domain Model

Reuse existing entities where they already express the correct concept. Add or reshape only the missing accounting concepts.

### Extend `FinanceAccount`

The account should gain explicit accounting attributes rather than relying only on free-form `AccountType`:

- Account class: asset, liability, equity, income, expense.
- Normal balance: debit or credit.
- Active date range and posting-enabled state.
- Control-account classification, such as accounts receivable, accounts payable, bank, tax receivable, or tax payable.
- Default tax code where appropriate.
- Statement classification/mapping.
- Template provenance and version.
- Optional manual-posting restriction for control accounts.

Account codes must be unique per company. Accounts referenced by posted entries cannot be deleted; they can only be deactivated for future posting.

### Add accounting settings

Create a tenant-owned aggregate for:

- Legal entity name and registration/tax identifiers.
- Base currency.
- Accounting-policy pack and chart-template version.
- Fiscal-year pattern.
- Rounding mode and tolerance.
- Default receivables, payables, bank, retained earnings, tax, rounding, and exchange-gain/loss accounts.
- Approval thresholds and manual-journal policy.
- Current setup state and accountant validation state.

Settings that affect posted history must be versioned or have effective dates. Changing a default changes future postings only.

### Add voucher series and number allocation

Represent voucher series explicitly, for example general journal, customer invoice, supplier bill, bank, and corrections. Store the next number per company, fiscal year, and series behind a database concurrency boundary. The human-readable entry number can then be composed from series, year, and allocated sequence while the GUID remains the technical identity.

### Add tax codes and tax posting details

A tax code should contain:

- Stable code and plain-English name.
- Tax model and classification, such as purchase/recoverable tax, sales/payable tax, inclusive tax, exclusive tax, exempt, reverse charge, or another pack-defined type.
- Effective date range.
- Ledger accounts used by the posting template.
- Reporting category mapping for the selected accounting-policy pack.
- Rounding behavior.

Persist calculated tax details on the source document and posted journal lines so a later configuration change cannot rewrite historical meaning.

### Harden journal entries

Extend the journal model as needed with:

- Voucher series and sequence number.
- Document date, posting date, and optional due date distinction.
- Base currency and optional source-currency totals/exchange rate.
- Posting reason/type.
- Source version and idempotency key.
- Reversal relationship and correction reason.
- Posted-by actor and approval reference.
- Row/concurrency version.

The journal aggregate or posting policy must validate the complete entry. Do not rely on constructing individually valid lines and assume the total entry is balanced.

### Preserve source documents

Use the existing document/link infrastructure for invoice images, PDFs, imported statements, and supporting files. Persist content hashes and immutable references for evidence used by a posted entry. Provider payloads may be retained as integration evidence, but provider JSON must not become the accounting system of record.

## Architecture and Ownership

Follow `production-implementation.md`, `architecture-inst.md`, and `docs/architecture-rules.MD`. UI work must also follow `ui-instructions.md` and `docs/design.md`.

Place work in the existing modular-monolith boundaries:

- `VirtualCompany.Domain`: accounting entities, value objects, and invariant-preserving state transitions.
- `VirtualCompany.Application/Finance`: typed commands, queries, policies, result contracts, and capability interfaces.
- `VirtualCompany.Persistence`: EF configurations and shared SQL Server persistence.
- `VirtualCompany.Persistence.Migrations`: schema migrations and model snapshot.
- `VirtualCompany.Infrastructure.Finance`: posting, reconciliation, close, reporting, and provider adapter implementations.
- `VirtualCompany.Api`: authorization, company context, request mapping, and safe problem responses only.
- `VirtualCompany.Web`: Blazor pages, presentation models, and typed API clients only.

Do not put EF queries or accounting decisions in controllers or Razor components. Do not create a sibling accounting implementation that competes with the existing Finance module. Fortnox and all other providers remain optional adapters, not owners of internal accounting policy.

### Posting command model

Implement the central posting boundary defined in the architecture decision with commands and results conceptually shaped as:

```text
ValidatePosting(command)
  -> PostingPreview

Post(command)
  -> PostedJournalEntry

Reverse(postedEntry, reason)
  -> ReversalJournalEntry
```

Source-specific services build proposed entries through versioned posting templates. The central service then performs tenant, period, account, tax, balance, approval, idempotency, and sequence checks in one database transaction.

All automatic journal creation—including current bank and cash-settlement posting—should converge on this boundary. Existing service facades can remain for compatibility, but they must not keep separate accounting rules.

### Commands and queries

Commands own state changes:

- Configure accounting.
- Create/update a draft journal.
- Preview/post/reverse a journal.
- Register/post/credit an invoice or bill.
- Import a bank statement.
- Reconcile a transaction.
- Validate/close/reopen a period.

Queries produce purpose-built read models:

- Chart of accounts.
- Journal list/detail.
- Account ledger.
- Trial balance.
- Receivables/payables aging.
- Configured tax summary.
- Profit and loss statement.
- Balance sheet.
- Period-close status.

Read models must derive financial truth from posted journal lines. Operational invoice/bill/payment tables provide workflow state and drill-down context, but must not become a second competing balance calculation.

## Posting Workflows

Posting templates must be configuration-aware, deterministic, versioned, and covered by accountant-reviewed examples.

### Customer invoice

For a basic taxable sale:

```text
Debit   Accounts receivable      gross amount
Credit  Sales revenue            net amount
Credit  Tax payable              tax amount
```

The selected tax code and policy-pack version determine whether tax is inclusive or exclusive, which account is used, and how the amount is reported. Posting changes the invoice from approved/draft to posted and records the journal link atomically. Sending an invoice is a separate external side effect with its own durable delivery workflow.

### Customer credit note

Create a credit document linked to the original invoice and post the inverse accounting effect. Do not delete or rewrite the original invoice or journal.

### Supplier bill

For a basic deductible purchase:

```text
Debit   Expense or asset         net amount
Debit   Recoverable tax          tax amount
Credit  Accounts payable        gross amount
```

The selected accounting-policy pack may instead classify tax as non-recoverable and include it in the expense/asset amount. The supplier-bill review may recommend the expense account and tax code. Posting requires deterministic validation and the configured approval. Duplicate supplier, invoice number, amount, currency, and date checks remain part of intake.

### Supplier credit note

Post the inverse of the applicable supplier-bill treatment and link it to the original bill.

### Payment and bank reconciliation

An incoming customer payment normally posts:

```text
Debit   Bank                     amount received
Credit  Accounts receivable      settled amount
```

An outgoing supplier payment normally posts:

```text
Debit   Accounts payable         settled amount
Credit  Bank                     amount paid
```

Fees, discounts, rounding, and exchange differences use explicit additional lines and configured accounts. The reconciliation transaction, payment allocation, source mapping, and journal link must commit atomically. Duplicate imports and retries must return the existing result.

The current fallback that chooses a generic offset account is useful simulation behavior but is not sufficient for an accounting product. Production reconciliation must use the matched receivable/payable, a reviewed categorization, or a suspense account that creates visible follow-up work.

### Manual journal

A user with the correct permission can prepare a draft containing:

- Posting date and period.
- Voucher series.
- Explanation.
- Two or more account lines.
- Debit/credit amounts.
- Tax code and optional supported dimensions.
- Evidence attachment.

The UI continuously shows debit total, credit total, and difference, but the backend repeats all validation. Posting a manual journal above the configured risk threshold creates an approval rather than accepting a client-supplied approval flag.

### Correction and reversal

A posted entry exposes `Create correction`, not `Edit` or `Delete`. A full reversal creates equal opposite lines and links both entries. An adjusting entry contains only the required difference but still references the original and records the reason. Corrections in a later open period must never mutate a locked period.

### Period close

The existing close capability should become an accountant-facing checklist. At minimum it blocks close for:

- Unposted source documents within the period.
- Unbalanced or invalid journals.
- Missing required statement mappings.
- Unresolved reconciliation conflicts or suspense items.
- Incomplete tax review where configured as required.
- Missing control-account reconciliation.

The validation result shows record links and plain-English remediation. Close and reporting lock are separate audited actions if the existing model continues to distinguish them.

## API and Authorization

Use existing company-scoped routes and policies. Add capability-specific permissions such as:

- View accounting.
- Prepare accounting drafts.
- Post routine source documents.
- Post manual journals.
- Approve accounting postings.
- Reverse posted entries.
- Close or reopen periods.
- Manage accounting settings.
- Export books.

Every write must check both membership and resource ownership server-side. High-impact actions must use trusted actor identity, current approval state, and content/source version. The client cannot assert that it is autonomous, approved, or authorized.

Return stable reason codes and safe explanations for conflicts such as closed period, unbalanced entry, duplicate posting, inactive account, stale source version, missing approval, or cross-company resource.

## User Interface

Reuse the established Finance design and navigation. The first release needs a compact set of accountant-oriented surfaces:

### Accounting setup

- A default-first micro-company setup flow for fiscal year, base currency, and a simple chart of accounts.
- Optional selection of a validated country/region accounting-policy pack; otherwise the company uses the clearly labelled country-neutral ledger mode.
- Review of default/control accounts and configured tax behavior.
- Validation summary with no silent defaults for legally important choices.

### Chart of accounts

- Searchable account list with code, name, class, status, tax default, and statement mapping.
- Add/deactivate account actions subject to policy.
- Clear indication when an account is protected because it has posted activity.

### Journal

- Filterable voucher list with number, date, status, source, description, debit/credit total, and correction state.
- Journal detail with lines, source record, document evidence, approval, actor, audit timeline, and reversal links.
- Manual-journal workbench with a persistent balance indicator and explicit preview before posting.

### Receivables and payables

- Reuse the existing invoice, bill, payment, and review pages.
- Add visible accounting state: not ready, awaiting approval, ready to post, posted, reversed, or blocked with a plain-English reason.
- Provide a direct drill-down from a document to its posting.

### Banking and reconciliation

- Import status and duplicate summary.
- Unmatched, partially matched, matched, posted, and conflict queues.
- Side-by-side bank transaction and candidate invoice/bill/payment evidence.
- Explicit suspense handling and later reclassification.

### Reports and close

- General ledger by account and period.
- Trial balance with opening, debit, credit, and closing columns.
- Profit and loss and balance sheet with drill-down to journal lines.
- Configured tax summary with tax-code and source-document drill-down.
- Period-close checklist and lock history.

All pages need loading, empty, validation, conflict, unauthorized, blocked, success, and recoverable-failure states. User-facing language must remain plain English and must not expose enum names or provider internals.

## External Accounting Providers and Exports

The primary target is a micro-company with no accounting application. For that target, Virtual Company is the accounting system of record and must work without an external provider.

An optional provider integration needs an explicit operating mode:

1. **Internal-ledger authority:** the normal mode. Virtual Company owns postings and may export or synchronize approved results to an accountant, tax tool, bank, or accounting provider.
2. **External-provider authority:** an optional compatibility mode for a company that later adopts or already has another accounting system. Virtual Company imports results for visibility but does not claim its internal ledger is authoritative for the same periods.
3. **Migration/cutover:** one authority is selected for each period; dual writes are avoided while historical data is reconciled.

Fortnox is one possible adapter, not a dependency or a preferred accounting model. Do not allow two systems to independently create authoritative postings for the same business event. Persist external references and synchronization state. External writes use the existing approval, durable background execution/outbox, idempotency, bounded retry, and reconciliation patterns. Ambiguous provider outcomes become operator-visible reconciliation work; they are not blindly retried.

The country-neutral export should always provide a documented machine-readable journal, account, period, party, tax, currency, source-reference, and attachment manifest. Country packs may add local accountant or authority formats without changing the underlying books.

## Database and Migration

Schema changes require EF Core migrations in `VirtualCompany.Persistence.Migrations` and an updated snapshot. SQL Server is the schema authority. The same migration history must support local SQL Server and Docker SQL Server restore/run flows.

A safe rollout should:

1. Add new nullable/versioned accounting fields and new tables without breaking existing readers.
2. Seed the country-neutral defaults and versioned accounting-policy packs, tax codes, and voucher series idempotently.
3. Backfill account classifications and source versions in bounded, resumable jobs.
4. Detect rather than guess ambiguous historical account or posting mappings.
5. Reconcile historical entries by company, period, source, and amount.
6. Produce a cutover report listing accepted mappings, conflicts, skipped records, and totals.
7. For companies with legacy data, enable authoritative internal posting only after opening balances and trial balances agree with the selected source. New micro-companies can start from a zero or explicitly entered opening balance.
8. Enforce stronger non-null and uniqueness constraints only after backfill verification.

Never repair this model with startup DDL, `EnsureCreated`, database recreation, or destructive migration shortcuts.

## Audit, Observability, and Recovery

Business audit and technical logs are separate.

For each posting, reversal, approval, settings change, import, reconciliation, close, reopen, export, and provider write, retain:

- Company and actor.
- Action and target.
- Source and source version.
- Outcome and stable reason code.
- Approval and policy decision where applicable.
- Journal and external references.
- Correlation identifier and timestamp.
- Safe before/after evidence.

Operational signals should include posting successes/failures, duplicate/idempotent replays, unmatched bank items, suspense balance, reconciliation conflicts, stale approvals, close blockers, provider retry/reconciliation backlog, and report-generation failures.

Backups must include the SQL Server database and source-document/object storage as one recoverable accounting evidence set. Restore verification should prove that journal lines, source links, documents, audit evidence, and provider references remain consistent.

## Implementation Sequence

### Phase 0: Define the country-agnostic kernel and policy-pack contract

Separate universal accounting invariants from configurable local policy. Define the versioned pack schema, validation boundary, upgrade behavior, fallback behavior, and compliance labelling. Confirm that the internal ledger is authoritative by default. Inventory existing company data only for tenants that already contain finance or provider-derived records.

**Exit gate:** the kernel contains no country, currency, account-code, tax-type, or provider assumptions; the policy-pack contract is reviewed; and every existing company has a migration/cutover decision where needed.

### Phase 1: Harden the ledger core

Implement explicit account semantics, voucher series, atomic number allocation, whole-entry balance validation, immutable posted state, reversal links, source versions, idempotency, actor/approval evidence, and row concurrency. Route existing bank and cash-settlement posting through the central boundary.

**Exit gate:** no application path can create an unbalanced entry, alter a posted entry, post to another tenant or closed period, or duplicate a source posting.

### Phase 2: Complete micro-company setup, accounts, periods, and manual journals

Add the short default-first onboarding flow, accounting settings, country-neutral defaults, optional versioned country packs, chart/tax templates, fiscal-period management, account administration, journal queries, the manual-journal workbench, posting preview, approval, and reversal.

**Exit gate:** a new micro-company without any external accounting system can configure its books and safely post, inspect, approve, and reverse a manual journal.

### Phase 3: Make invoices and bills ledger-backed

Implement versioned customer invoice, supplier bill, and credit-note posting templates. Connect existing intake, extraction, review, duplicate detection, approvals, source documents, and operational status to the authoritative journal link.

**Exit gate:** every posted receivable/payable document has exactly one traceable accounting effect, and document totals reconcile to the corresponding control account.

### Phase 4: Complete banking and reconciliation

Harden statement import, duplicate detection, matching, partial allocation, fees/differences, suspense handling, cash posting, correction, and conflict queues.

**Exit gate:** imported transactions can be retried safely, matched amounts cannot exceed source amounts, and bank/control-account reconciliations explain every difference.

### Phase 5: Complete reports, configurable tax summaries, and close

Make posted journal lines the common source for the general ledger, trial balance, statements, configured tax summary, and close validation. Add the country-neutral accountant export, pack-provided export formats, and drill-down evidence.

**Exit gate:** reports agree with the trial balance, statement drill-down reaches immutable source evidence, and a period cannot close with configured blockers.

### Phase 6: Migrate and cut over safely

Run resumable backfills for companies with existing finance data, reconcile opening/historical balances against the selected source, resolve conflicts, test SQL Server backup/restore, and then enable per company after sign-off. New companies start directly in internal-ledger authority mode and do not require a provider cutover.

**Exit gate:** the cutover report balances, recovery is proven, and there is no dual-authority posting window.

## Minimum Acceptance Criteria

- **Given** journal lines whose debits and credits differ, **when** posting is requested, **then** the backend rejects the entry and persists no voucher number or posting.
- **Given** a posted entry, **when** any caller attempts to edit or delete it, **then** the operation is rejected and the correction workflow is offered.
- **Given** two concurrent posts for the same source version, **when** both execute, **then** one posting is committed and both callers resolve to the same journal entry.
- **Given** concurrent postings in one voucher series, **when** numbers are allocated, **then** committed vouchers have unique monotonically increasing numbers without reuse.
- **Given** a closed or locked period, **when** a normal or privileged posting is attempted, **then** it is rejected unless a separately authorized reopen workflow completes.
- **Given** an account, source record, or period from another company, **when** it is supplied to a command, **then** no data is disclosed or changed.
- **Given** an approved source document that changes after approval, **when** posting is attempted, **then** the stale approval/source version is rejected.
- **Given** a posted customer invoice, **when** its journal is reviewed, **then** receivable equals gross, revenue plus configured payable tax equals gross, and the source document is reachable.
- **Given** a posted supplier bill with recoverable tax, **when** its journal is reviewed, **then** expense/asset plus configured recoverable tax equals payable and the source document is reachable.
- **Given** two contrasting accounting-policy packs, **when** the same neutral business scenarios are processed, **then** the kernel preserves identical balance, immutability, authorization, idempotency, and audit invariants while each pack supplies its own accounts, taxes, terms, and reports.
- **Given** no country pack is selected, **when** the company uses the ledger, **then** basic bookkeeping remains available and country-specific compliance reports are clearly marked unavailable rather than guessed.
- **Given** an idempotently re-imported bank statement, **when** processing completes, **then** no bank transaction, allocation, or journal is duplicated.
- **Given** a provider timeout with an unknown result, **when** the worker handles it, **then** the action becomes reconciliation-required and is not blindly repeated.
- **Given** all close checks pass, **when** an authorized user closes and locks a period, **then** reports are reproducible from the locked posted ledger and later corrections occur in an open period.
- **Given** a database and document-store restore, **when** integrity verification runs, **then** ledger totals, source links, audit references, and evidence hashes remain valid.

## Verification

Verification should be proportional to the financial and multi-tenant risk:

- Domain tests for account, line, entry, period, reversal, tax, and sequence invariants.
- Finance-module tests for every posting template and rounding edge case.
- SQL Server integration tests for transactions, uniqueness, concurrency, idempotency, sequence allocation, and migration upgrades.
- Authorization and cross-tenant tests for every command and sensitive query.
- Approval-version tests proving changed content cannot use stale approval.
- Failure-injection tests for duplicate delivery, database conflict, process restart, provider timeout, provider-success/local-failure, and report regeneration.
- Contract/API tests for stable request, response, and problem details.
- Blazor component tests for setup, journal, reconciliation, reports, and close states.
- Screenshot-first browser verification for new or materially changed UI according to `ui-instructions.md` and `docs/design.md`.
- Full API/Web builds and the broader relevant regression suite.
- Migration tests against both an empty SQL Server database and a restored representative database.
- Docker SQL Server restore, migration, startup, smoke, backup, and restore verification.
- Kernel tests using at least two deliberately contrasting synthetic policy packs to detect hidden country assumptions.
- Locally qualified reviewer-approved golden examples for every policy pack advertised as country-compliant, with known journal, tax, trial-balance, profit-and-loss, and balance-sheet results.

## Definition of Done

The bare-bones accounting application is complete only when the internal ledger has one declared authority, all in-scope business events post through the governed accounting boundary, reports reconcile to posted journal lines, posted history is immutable and correctable, tenant/authorization/approval rules are enforced in the backend, migrations and Docker restore paths are proven, and operators can diagnose and recover failures.

There must be no production scaffolding, mock production data, silent fallback to simulation, unhandled intermediate state, direct provider write from a request handler, startup schema DDL, or deferred in-scope TODO. AI recommendations must remain evidence-grounded suggestions inside deterministic accounting and approval controls.
