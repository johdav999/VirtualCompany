# Native receivables Release 2 reference prompts

These reference prompts were written before the Prompt 9 UI implementation. They follow `docs/design.md` and `ui-instructions.md`; the generated images are design references only and must not be served as product assets.

## Native invoice editor and issue flow

Use case: ui-mockup. Asset type: high-fidelity desktop SaaS product screenshot. Create the “New customer invoice” editor and issue-review flow for the existing Virtual Company Finance product. Preserve the current product shell: fixed white left sidebar, canonical Finance navigation, light `#F7F9FC` workspace, Inter-style typography, blue `#2563EB` accents, white 14–16px rounded cards, soft borders, restrained shadows, and Laura as the contextual Finance Manager. At 1536×1024, show a practical three-column operational layout: a compact draft step rail for Details, Lines, Review, Approval, Issue; a large editor workspace; and a narrower server-preview/attention panel. The editor must show customer selection, issue and due dates, structured payment terms, buyer reference, currency, evidence, and a readable line-items table with description, quantity, unit price, discount, VAT treatment, and dimensions. Show controls to add and reorder lines with obvious keyboard focus. The preview panel must clearly say “Calculated by Virtual Company” and display net, VAT, rounding, and total from the server, plus one amber blocker with plain remediation and cited evidence. Show approval as “Waiting for approval”, an explicit issue confirmation area that explains numbering and journal posting, and a primary “Issue invoice” button that is unavailable until approval is current. Include a compact success progression for number, journal, PDF generation, and queued email without claiming final delivery. Include loading, validation, stale-preview, and narrow responsive behavior where natural. Use realistic English product copy. Avoid raw reason codes, tax keys, hashes, provider payloads, tenant jargon, client-calculated authoritative totals, fake success, simulation controls, gradients, concept-art styling, or watermark.

## Consolidated receivables and collections workspace

Use case: ui-mockup. Asset type: high-fidelity desktop SaaS product screenshot. Create the consolidated “Receivables” workspace inside the existing Virtual Company Finance product. Preserve the fixed white app sidebar and canonical Finance section navigation, light `#F7F9FC` background, Inter-style typography, blue primary accents, white rounded cards, soft borders, restrained shadows, and Laura’s grounded contextual presence. At 1536×1024, show a calm operational control center with a page header and primary “New invoice” action; four KPI cards for Outstanding, Overdue, Due this week, and Promises due; then accessible pills for Invoices, Drafts, Recurring, Collections, and Customers. Use a 60/40 list-detail layout. The left card contains a prioritized collections queue with search and filters, readable customer/invoice rows, amounts, age, owner, friendly channel-specific states such as “Email delivered”, “Delivery needs checking”, “Promise due”, and one clear next action. The selected detail panel shows current balance, aging, invoice/statement links, delivery attempts, payments and allocations, dispute or promise details, owner/tasks, and a chronological timeline. Include controls for creating a statement, preparing a reminder, recording a dispute or promise, and safe credit/refund/write-off actions, each using confirmation and backend-provided availability explanations. Show recurring billing summary cards with next occurrence, pause/resume/end, and blocked occurrence remediation. Keep Laura visible with a concise recommendation and explicit “Data used” links; she recommends but does not execute sensitive actions. Include an honest unavailable e-invoice state with email/PDF fallback, production empty/loading/error/retry states, keyboard focus, and a narrow responsive preview inset. Avoid raw statuses, internal identifiers, policy names, provider payloads, mock production data, generic admin-console density, decorative charts, futuristic styling, gradients, or watermark.

## Receivables production operations and readiness

Use case: ui-mockup. Asset type: high-fidelity desktop SaaS product screenshot. Create the “Receivables operations” view inside the existing Virtual Company Finance receivables workspace. Preserve the fixed white app sidebar, canonical Finance navigation, the established receivables tabs, light `#F7F9FC` background, Inter-style typography, blue `#2563EB` accents, white 12–16px rounded cards, soft borders, restrained shadows, and calm operational language. At 1536×1024, show a page header with overall state “Release blocked” and a precise explanation that external proof is still required. Below it, show three compact summary cards for Blocking issues, Needs attention, and Healthy checks, followed by a dense but readable two-column operator queue. Each check card must have a friendly title, status pill, bounded count or amount, one-sentence evidence summary, and a direct remediation action. Include the complete categories: stale approvals, number gaps, PDF render failures, email delivery ambiguity, blocked recurring runs, rejected e-invoices, refunds needing reconciliation, accounts-receivable control difference, overdue collection follow-ups, and document/archive failures. Show representative honest states: an amber number gap with a documented reason, red delivery ambiguity that says not to resend, a red AR control difference with a SEK amount, and healthy green states elsewhere. Include an “Evidence and recovery” side panel linking to migration status, restore verification, supported-volume profile, and release evidence, plus a timestamp and explicit note that provider acceptance is not recipient delivery. Provide accessible keyboard focus, loading/error/retry and empty-state intent, and a narrow responsive preview inset. Avoid raw reason codes, internal statuses, hashes, provider payloads, customer personal data, decorative charts, generic admin-console density, gradients, fake success, certification claims, or watermark.
